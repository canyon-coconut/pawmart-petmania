Feeding

How much should you actually feed your dog?

The chart on the bag is written for an average dog. Yours is not average — here is the method we use at the counter, with a bowl and a bit of honesty.

Start with the bag. Weigh your dog (bathroom scales, hold-and-subtract works fine), find their weight on the chart, and feed the lower end of the range for the first two weeks. The chart assumes a dog that is entire, active and living outdoors in winter. A neutered dog who supervises the sofa needs less — sometimes a lot less.

Then watch the dog, not the bag

Run both hands along their ribs. You should feel the ribs easily under a thin layer of fat, like the back of your hand. From above, there should be a waist behind the ribs; from the side, the tummy should tuck up. If you have to press to find ribs, the bowl is too full. If you can count ribs from across the room, it is not full enough.

Three things that change the number

  • Treats count. Training treats, dental sticks, the corner of your toast — keep the whole lot under about a tenth of the day's food, and take it out of the bowl, not on top of it.
  • Age counts. Puppies eat surprisingly much for their size because they are building a dog. Seniors slow down and need less of everything except protein quality.
  • Neutering counts. Most dogs need roughly a fifth less after neutering. Nobody tells you this at the vet's office door, but the scales will tell you by Christmas.

Change it slowly, measure honestly

Adjust by about a tenth at a time — never halve a bowl overnight — and weigh again in two weeks. Changing foods entirely? Mix the new food in over a week: a quarter, a half, three quarters, done. A sudden swap is the most common cause of the 2am garden trip we hear about on Mondays.

The counter rule: can't feel the ribs, cut a tenth. Can see the ribs, add a tenth. Everything else is fine-tuning.

Still not sure? Weight checks at our Tuesday clinic are free — walk in, hop on the big scales, and we will help you work out a number for your actual dog, not the average one.
