Five signs your cat is bored

An indoor cat's whole world is your house. When that world gets dull, they don't write a complaint — they redecorate. Five signs we hear about at the counter, and what actually fixes them.

1. The 3am zoomies

Cats are built to hunt at dawn and dusk. A cat who has slept eighteen hours straight will spend that energy somewhere, and your sleeping face is apparently a landmark. A proper play session in the evening — ten minutes with a wand toy, ending with a small meal — moves the explosion to a civilised hour.

2. Ankle ambushes

Pouncing on moving feet is hunting practice, not spite. Redirect it: wand toys and kickers give the ambush a legal target. Punishing it just teaches the cat that feet are dangerous and interesting.

3. Grooming bald patches

Over-grooming the belly or legs is a classic stress-and-boredom signal. One caution first: if it starts suddenly, or the skin is sore, that is a vet question before it is a toy question — our Tuesday clinic can take a first look.

4. Raiding bins and cupboards

A cat opening cupboards is a cat solving puzzles you didn't set. Set some: kibble in a puzzle feeder or a cardboard box maze beats an expensive toy most days, because the prize is food and the puzzle changes.

5. Sleeping even more than usual

Cats sleep a lot — but a cat who stops claiming the sunny spots, the window and your laundry pile is often a cat who has given up on the day containing anything. New perches and a rotating toy box (three toys out, the rest hidden, swap weekly) restart the patrol instinct.

The short version: boredom looks like naughtiness. It is usually a schedule problem, not a cat problem — two ten-minute play sessions and a perch with a view fix more than any single toy.
